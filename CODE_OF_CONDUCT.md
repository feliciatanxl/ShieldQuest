# Code of Conduct

Project SHIELD: ShieldQuest is dedicated to empowering young people to make safe, responsible choices and look out for one another. That same standard of mutual care, respect, and psychological safety governs how we interact as a community—including our core team, contributors, scenario reviewers, educators, workshop facilitators, and youth participants.

---

## Our Pledge

We pledge to provide a respectful, welcoming, inclusive, and harassment-free environment for everyone participating in ShieldQuest, regardless of age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity and expression, level of experience, socioeconomic background, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, sexual orientation, or educational pathway.

Because Project SHIELD involves young people—including secondary school, JC, polytechnic, and ITE students—we hold ourselves to the highest ethical and safeguarding standards expected in youth-development and educational settings.

---

## Expected Behaviour

All participants, contributors, and facilitators are expected to:

- **Prioritise Youth Wellbeing:** Ensure all scenarios, interactions, and debriefing discussions foster emotional safety, empowerment, and resilience.
- **Communicate with Kindness and Respect:** Offer constructive critique focused on ideas, code, and scenario pedagogy, rather than individuals.
- **Maintain Clear Adult–Youth Boundaries:** Maintain professional, transparent boundaries in all interactions involving youth co-designers or pilot participants. Adult contributors and facilitators must never engage in private messaging, solicit personal social media contacts, or establish unsupervised off-platform communication with minor participants.
- **Practise Empathy and Inclusion:** Respect diverse learning paces, cultural perspectives, and technical abilities across different age groups.
- **Respect Privacy by Default:** Uphold data minimisation principles across all communications, meetings, testing sessions, and repository contributions.

---

## Unacceptable Behaviour

The following behaviours are strictly prohibited across all project channels, repositories, workshops, and pilot sessions:

1. **Harassment and Discrimination:** Any form of verbal abuse, demeaning comments, intimidation, personal attacks, or discrimination based on personal characteristics or identity.
2. **Sexualised Language or Imagery:** Any sexualised comments, jokes, suggestions, or media. Any sexualised conduct or imagery involving minors is strictly illegal and will result in immediate termination of participation and formal reporting to law enforcement authorities.
3. **Victim-Blaming Attitudes:** Scams, theft, and peer exploitation are the fault of the perpetrator, never the victim. Mocking, shaming, or blaming individuals for being deceived, manipulated, or targeted is unacceptable in both gameplay discussions and contributor reviews.
4. **Coercing Personal Disclosures:** **No participant may ever be pressured to disclose personal experiences** of being scammed, harassed, bullied, or involved in an offence. Scenario discussions must remain grounded in fictional case studies and transferable principles.
5. **Doxxing or Sharing Identifiers:** Publishing or sharing private personal information (such as real names, school affiliations, NRIC/FIN, telephone numbers, residential addresses, or photographs) of participants or contributors without explicit written consent.
6. **Introducing Malicious or Instructional Material:** Committing or distributing real phishing URLs, functional scam scripts, malware, or operational instructions for committing illegal acts.
7. **Disruptive Facilitation:** Disrupting workshop environments or belittling participant questions during Think–Vote–Explain discussions.

## Safeguarding and Disclosure Protocols

During crime-prevention workshops or co-design activities, a participant may occasionally make a spontaneous disclosure regarding real-world harm, ongoing distress, or exploitation. All facilitators and contributors must adhere to the following protocol:

### Handling Participant Disclosures

- **Remain Calm and Supportive:** Listen without judging, interrogating, or displaying shock. Do not make unfulfillable promises of absolute secrecy, as statutory safeguarding duties take precedence.
- **Ensure Immediate Safety:** If a participant indicates they or someone else is in immediate physical danger, follow the emergency escalation process immediately.
- **Do Not Investigate:** Facilitators and contributors are not criminal investigators or clinical therapists. Do not probe for extraneous details or attempt to interrogate potential perpetrators.
- **Confidential Notification:** Report the disclosure immediately and privately to the designated Project Safeguarding Lead (Charlisa Tan) and the host institution's designated student welfare officer or teacher-in-charge.

## Scope and Enforcement

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces—including the GitHub repository, pull requests, issue trackers, project communication channels, co-design workshops, teacher briefings, and pilot delivery venues.

### Reporting Process

If you experience or witness behaviour that violates this Code of Conduct, or if you have concerns regarding youth safety, please report the matter privately to:

- **Repository Owner & Technical Lead:** Felicia Tan ([@feliciatanxl](https://github.com/feliciatanxl))
- **Content & Safeguarding Lead:** Charlisa Tan

All reports will be treated with confidentiality, reviewed promptly, and investigated fairly.

### Enforcement Actions

Team SecurePi reserves the right to take proportionate corrective actions, including:

1. **Correction:** A private written warning detailing the infraction and clarifying expected behaviour.
2. **Content Removal:** Deletion or redaction of violating scenario text, pull requests, comments, or repository issues.
3. **Temporary Suspension:** A temporary ban from participating in project repositories, discussions, or pilot workshops.
4. **Permanent Expulsion:** Permanent removal from the project, revocation of repository access, and termination of collaboration agreements. In cases involving severe safeguarding violations or child safety concerns, formal reports will be submitted to the relevant educational institutions and law enforcement agencies.
