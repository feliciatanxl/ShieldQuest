# ShieldQuest

**Choose Right. Protect Together.**
*Recognise the Risk. Make the Choice. Protect Your People.*

ShieldQuest is a mobile-first Progressive Web App (PWA) where young people practise the decisions that crime and scam prevention actually turn on. Players explore an interactive city board, investigate clues, vote with their squad, live with delayed consequences, and practise stepping in when a friend is at risk.

It is the youth-facing platform of **Project SHIELD**, a youth-led crime-prevention initiative by **Team SecurePi** (Nanyang Polytechnic, Singapore).

> ShieldQuest is a student project by Team SecurePi. It is not an official Singapore Police Force (SPF), National Crime Prevention Council (NCPC), National Youth Council (NYC) or Government product. All scenarios, brands, people, links and phone numbers in the game are fictional.

## Why ShieldQuest exists

Most crime-prevention education is episodic and passive: a talk, a poster, a video. The problem is rarely that youths do not know an action is wrong. It is whether they make the responsible choice when friends are watching, when the offer is urgent, or when the consequences feel distant.

In 2025 Singapore recorded **41,974 scam and cybercrime cases** with about **S$913.1 million** in losses, and **81.8%** of scam cases involved self-effected transfers, where victims were manipulated into moving the money themselves. Shop theft (4,109 cases) remained one of the top offences among youths arrested.
*Sources: Singapore Police Force, Annual Crime Brief 2025; Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025.*

ShieldQuest closes this **engagement gap** by making the decision itself the learning mechanic: players feel the temptation, make a choice, meet the consequences, reflect on the warning signs, and then practise protecting a peer.

ShieldQuest is Team SecurePi's entry to the **SPF / NCPC Delta Challenge 2026, Track B (Crime Prevention)**, delivered with the **NYC Young ChangeMakers (YCM)** grant.

## The S.H.I.E.L.D. framework

Every scenario reinforces one transferable decision-making process:

| Letter | Skill | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | Spot the Risk | Recognise warning signs, suspicious situations and risky peer behaviour. |
| **H** | Hold Before Acting | Pause before responding to pressure, urgency or temptation. |
| **I** | Identify the Influence | Consider who is influencing the decision and what they may gain. |
| **E** | Evaluate the Consequences | Think beyond immediate rewards and consider the impact. |
| **L** | Lead the Right Choice | Choose the safer, more responsible response. |
| **D** | Defend Your Community | Look out for peers and help stop risky behaviour from escalating. |

Because it teaches a process rather than a list of scam types, the framework stays relevant as scam tactics change.

## How a mission plays

| Stage | Player action | Learning function |
|---|---|---|
| 1.&nbsp;Explore | Navigate the city board (School Street, Retail District, Digi-District, Community Hub). | Places risk in familiar settings. |
| 2.&nbsp;Investigate | Inspect chats, images and clues. | Spot the Risk. |
| 3.&nbsp;Discuss | Think–Vote–Explain: vote privately, compare with the squad, discuss. | Surfaces peer norms and reasoning. |
| 4.&nbsp;Decide | Choose a response under pressure. | Hold Before Acting. |
| 5.&nbsp;Experience | See immediate **and delayed** consequences. | Makes trade-offs memorable. |
| 6.&nbsp;Protect | Apply the learning in Peer Shield Mode. | Builds bystander-intervention confidence. |

**Sample mission: "Easy Money".** A student is offered S$200 to receive money into their bank account and pass it on. The sender says it is legal, urgent and risk-free; a friend says "just try once". Accepting pays out immediately, then the Delayed Consequence Engine reverses the reward, drops the city's Trust Meter and flags the account. Asking for proof returns convincing but unverifiable screenshots. Rejecting, stopping contact and seeking trusted help protects the player and strengthens the VeriFox Guardian.

## Key features

- **Interactive city board** with short, investigative missions across four districts.
- **Delayed Consequence Engine:** short-term gains ("easy money") can trigger trust and risk penalties later.
- **Peer Shield Mode:** practise helping a friend who is being targeted or pressured.
- **Think–Vote–Explain squad play** for facilitated sessions, with rotating roles (Safety Lead, Evidence Checker, Peer Supporter).
- **Guardians earned through skill, never loot boxes:**

  | Guardian | Ability | Prevention skill |
  |---|---|---|
  | VeriFox | Checks a claim against trusted information | Verification before action |
  | Echo | Invites a teammate's explanation | Help-seeking and consultation |
  | Beacon | Identifies a safe reporting channel | Personal safety and escalation |
  | Shieldfin | Protects another player from a pressure event | Refusal skills and peer support |
  | Cluepaw | Reveals one overlooked clue | Situational awareness |
  | ByteBuddy | Highlights unsafe links, permissions or data requests | Cyber hygiene |

- **Scenario Management Portal** for educators, including a moderated Youth-Created Missions pipeline.
- **Anonymous analytics dashboard** tracking risk recognition and decision accuracy without profiling individuals.
- **Accessibility:** readable language, adjustable text size, colour-independent status cues, responsive layouts and low-bandwidth support.
- **Optional printable offline board-game kit** for low-tech sessions.

## Who it is for

Youths aged roughly **10–24**, with content bands chosen by educators:

| Segment | Age | Experience design | Example themes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary / early secondary | 10‑13 | Simple language, visual clues, 2–3 clear choices | Stranger approaches, game scams, cyberbullying, online safety |
| Secondary | 14‑16 | Team voting, branching outcomes, social pressure | Shop theft, phishing, online scams, harassment, risky dares |
| Post-secondary / tertiary | 17‑24 | Ambiguous evidence, competing priorities, financial risk | Job scams, money-mule recruitment, digital identity, suspicious financial requests |

The initial pilot prioritises secondary and post-secondary youths.

## Pilot plan

A six-month implementation, targeting about **200 youth participants**:

| Phase | Weeks | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Co-design | 1–3 | Youth validation, learning-outcome baseline |
| 2. Develop and expand MVP | 4–9 | Refined prototype, reviewed scenarios, scenario portal, offline kit |
| 3. Test and refine | 10–12 | Usability testing, accessibility fixes |
| 4. Controlled youth pilot | 13–18 | Facilitated sessions, starting at Nanyang Polytechnic |
| 5. Refined community pilot | 19–22 | Wider rollout and community activation |
| 6. Evaluation and handover | 23–24 | Impact report, facilitator toolkit, scale-up plan |

Each pilot session is a **90-minute facilitated workshop**: onboarding and pre-assessment (15 min), squad gameplay (45 min), facilitated debrief (20 min) and post-assessment (10 min).

## Measuring impact

1. **Risk recognition:** pre- and post-activity assessments.
2. **Decision accuracy:** unfamiliar scenarios test whether learning transfers.
3. **Consequence awareness:** recognition of delayed legal, financial and social consequences.
4. **Peer-intervention confidence:** confidence in redirecting a friend appropriately.
5. **Retention:** a 2–4 week follow-up where feasible.
6. **Engagement:** session and scenario completion, voluntary replay and participation time.

## Privacy and safety by design

- **No personal data.** Players are never asked for names, NRIC numbers, phone numbers, emails, banking details or Singpass data. Participation is anonymous or pseudonymous.
- **Random session codes** link pre- and post-assessments; results are reported only in aggregate.
- **No disclosure required.** Nobody has to share personal experiences of being scammed, bullied or involved in an offence, and nothing is scored on disclosures.
- **Offender-focused feedback.** Scenarios blame the manipulator's tactic, never the target, and every risky path has a recovery.
- **Moderated content.** Youth-submitted scenarios are template-based and need educator approval before publication.
- **Help is always close.** Players are pointed to trusted adults and official channels such as the SPF hotline (1800-255-0000), ScamShield (1799) and, in an emergency, 999.
- Designed in line with Singapore's **PDPA**.

## Project status

This repository is the home of the pilot-ready ShieldQuest platform. The earlier prototypes above are self-funded proofs of concept built by Team SecurePi; they have not yet been used with youth participants. Development of this version begins with the co-design phase.

**Before any pilot session:** every scenario needs two human sign-offs (content and safeguarding), and every statistic, helpline and legal statement must be re-checked against official Singapore sources.
